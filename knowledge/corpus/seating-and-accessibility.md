# Seating plans and guest accessibility

A thoughtful seating plan keeps the reception comfortable and helps guests enjoy
themselves. Group people who know each other or share interests, seat older
relatives away from loud speakers, and place a welcoming mix at any table of
guests who arrive not knowing others. Assigned seating or at least assigned
tables reduces confusion and speeds up dinner service.

Plan for accessibility from the start. Ensure step-free routes to the ceremony,
reception, and restrooms for guests who use wheelchairs or walkers, reserve
accessible parking close to the entrance, and leave wide aisles between tables.
Offer seating at the ceremony for anyone who cannot stand for long.

Ask about dietary needs on the RSVP so the caterer can prepare vegetarian, vegan,
allergy-safe, and other required meals, and clearly label buffet items. For
guests traveling with children, a small kids' area or activity packs can make a
long reception easier. Communicating logistics in advance, such as transport and
timing, helps every guest participate fully.
