# Wedding photography and videography

Photography and videography are among the few wedding investments you keep long
after the day, so choose these vendors thoughtfully and book them early. Review a
photographer's complete galleries from real weddings, not just portfolio
highlights, to judge how they handle a full day: getting ready, the ceremony,
low-light receptions, and candid moments. Make sure their style, whether bright
and airy, dark and moody, or classic and true-to-color, matches what you want.

Discuss coverage and deliverables clearly. Confirm how many hours are included,
whether a second shooter is available, the expected turnaround time for edited
images, how many photos you receive, and whether you get print or digital usage
rights. For video, ask about the length of the highlight film, whether a full
ceremony edit is included, and how audio from vows and speeches is captured.

Provide a shot list of must-have moments and key people, and share the day-of
timeline so the photographer can plan for good light, especially for portraits
near sunset. A pre-wedding engagement session is a good way to get comfortable in
front of the camera and to confirm you work well together before the big day.
