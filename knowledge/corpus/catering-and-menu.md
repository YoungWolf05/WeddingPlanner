# Wedding catering and menu planning

Food and drink are usually the largest single expense and a highlight guests
remember, so plan the menu with both taste and logistics in mind. Choose a
service style that fits your venue and budget: a plated dinner feels formal and
controls portions, a buffet offers variety and a relaxed pace, and stations or
family-style service encourage mingling. Passed appetizers during cocktail hour
keep guests happy while portraits are taken.

Always schedule a tasting before you commit, and use it to finalize dishes,
portion sizes, and presentation. Offer at least one vegetarian option and plan
for common allergies and dietary needs collected from the RSVPs. Confirm whether
service charges, staffing, rentals, and gratuities are included in the per-person
price or billed separately.

For drinks, decide between an open bar, a limited bar with beer, wine, and a
signature cocktail, or a dry celebration, and check the venue's corkage rules if
you can supply your own. Give the caterer a final confirmed headcount by the
agreed deadline, and provide the day-of timeline so dinner service lands at the
right moment.
